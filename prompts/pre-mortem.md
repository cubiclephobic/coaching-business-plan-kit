# Prompt ● The Pre-Mortem

Run this before you commit to a quarter, not after something breaks.

**What it does:** makes you less certain and more specific about what could go wrong. In the one controlled test we could find, a pre-mortem lowered people's confidence in a plan more than critiquing it or listing pros and cons did. No study shows that pre-mortemed plans fail less often, so run it for the specificity, not for luck.

Paste your filled `plan.md` after the prompt.

---

```
You are reviewing a business plan for a solo coaching practice. Your job is to argue the failure case, not to be encouraging.

It is twelve months from today. This plan clearly failed. The practice is in worse shape than when the plan was written.

Write the story of how that happened. Rules:

1. Name specific mechanisms, not categories. "Clients churned" is useless. "Her two largest clients both renewed in the same month, and the plan assumed both would renew" is what I want.
2. Find at least three failure paths that are not obvious from the plan's own wording. Look for things the plan never mentions: concentration risk, a single source of enquiries, capacity that cannot absorb the growth it forecasts, a price floor that quietly moved, an assumption the plan treats as a fact.
3. For each failure path, say which specific line of the plan it attacks, and what the earliest observable warning sign would have been.
4. Rank the failure paths by how likely they are, and say why in one line each.
5. End with the single change to this plan that would remove the most risk. One change.

Do not suggest I write a longer plan. Do not reassure me. Do not list generic business risks.

```

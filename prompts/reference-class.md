# Prompt ● Reference-Class Forecast

Use this instead of setting a target from ambition.

**What it does:** starts your forecast from what has happened to you before, rather than from the story you are telling yourself about next quarter. The method comes from Bent Flyvbjerg's work on forecasting large projects: ignore the narrative of the case in front of you, find comparable past cases, and begin from their real outcomes.

That research base is public infrastructure, not solo coaching practices. Applying it to your own quarters is our translation, not a published finding. It is still a better starting point than a number you picked because it felt motivating.

You need your real numbers from the last four to eight quarters or months. Not estimates.

---

```
You are helping me forecast next quarter for a solo coaching practice, using reference-class forecasting.

Below is my own history: the last [N] quarters of real numbers, plus what I predicted at the start of each one where I have it.

Do this, in order:

1. Treat my own past quarters as the reference class. Ignore my plans and intentions entirely for now.
2. For each number (enquiries, discovery calls, conversion rate, new clients, revenue), give me the median, the range, and the trend across those quarters.
3. Tell me my forecast error: where I predicted and what happened, as a ratio. If I consistently over-forecast by a factor, name the factor.
4. Give me a base-rate forecast for next quarter from that history alone, with a plausible range rather than one number.
5. Only now, look at my plan for next quarter. Tell me what specifically would have to change for the outcome to beat the base rate, and whether anything in the plan actually does that.
6. Flag any number where I have too little history for a base rate to mean anything. Say so plainly rather than inventing one.

Be blunt about the gap between the base rate and my target. Do not split the difference to be encouraging.

Here is my history:
```

---

**After it answers:** put the base-rate range in section 4 of your plan next to your target. At the quarterly review you will have both, and you will learn which one you should have trusted.
